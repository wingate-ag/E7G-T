The practical sequence.

Use the smallest profile that changes the next responsible move without hiding risk.

1. Frame

Name the entity, semantic context, inquiry, model boundary, and intended use.

2. View

Declare viewing or projection, preserved structure, loss, distortion, and source candidates.

3. Define sameness

State tolerated differences and the relations, invariants, behaviours, or interfaces that define the phase.

4. Test sufficiency

Determine whether the reconstruction fibre maps to one phase, several phases, none, or an unknown set.

5. Navigate

Inspect typed transitions, phase boundaries, path choice, operation order, and representative dependence.

6. Act or stop

Classify, narrow, source-return, obtain another view, compare paths, repair, rely within scope, or stop.

Where the phase layer matters.

AI answers

Different source and support states can produce the same fluent answer while belonging to different reliance phases.

Translation QA

Different wording may be harmless variation—or may cross a legal-force, identity, or usability boundary.

Dashboards

The same green tile may be compatible with source configurations in different readiness or health phases.

Software

A refactoring claim requires behavioural equivalence, representative checks, and path-sensitive validation.

Proof work

A visible proof state may not uniquely determine proof readiness or discharged-obligation status.

Documents and decisions

A summary, status, or approval-looking view may not carry the phase required for operational reliance.

Public caution

E7G-T is not established mathematics as a whole, empirical physics, a proof system, an authority decision, or a replacement for domain expertise. Formal-looking notation does not create evidence, proof, authorisation, validation, or performed work.

The view may hide the source. The source candidates may span different phases. Responsible reliance begins when both uncertainties are controlled.

# APP-002 — Translation as Projection and Phase Control

Translation remains an invariant-preserving cross-language representation task, but the unified kernel distinguishes ordinary variation from operationally material phase change.

## Core question

> Which target-language differences are tolerated for this purpose, and which edit changes legal force, documentary identity, evidential posture, usability, or another protected condition?

## Typical phase criteria

- identity preservation;
- dates, numbers, names, and references;
- legal modality and obligation strength;
- documentary status and source annotations;
- register and intended audience;
- formatting that carries operational meaning;
- certification or self-certification boundaries.

## Example

`may terminate` and `has the option to terminate` may be phase-equivalent under a legal-modality criterion.

`shall terminate` may cross the phase boundary because discretionary force becomes mandatory.

## Combined review

1. Account for the source-to-target projection and its losses.
2. Define the inquiry and phase criterion.
3. Compare target candidates under the protected conditions.
4. Identify whether the change is linguistic variation, configuration revision, retargeting, or operational phase transition.
5. Require source return when a visible target cannot determine the source force uniquely.
